1. Neutral chain and ideals
Every n in N lies neither above u or y nor strictly below either: a strict predecessor would belong to their common strict downset D. Thus n is incomparable with both. Two incomparable N-points together with u,y would be a four-element antichain. The width<=3 implication is valid.
D is an ideal. No N-point can precede a D-point, and no point of either upward set can precede an N-point. Once N is a chain, these observations prove that D union N_j is an ideal, including j=0 and R. Relations D<N are allowed and do not invalidate the argument. Arbitrary compatible successor relations are also allowed.

2. Prefix counts and Stanley realization
For j>=1, n_j is maximal in D union N_j: it cannot precede D and is the last point of its chain. Appending n_j gives a bijection from LE(D union N_(j-1)) to extensions of D union N_j ending in n_j. These are precisely the extensions ending in N. Hence c_j counts extensions ending in D; c_0=e(D) when D is nonempty. Every such ordered prefix can be followed by every extension of Q_j because its underlying set is an ideal. For empty D only the empty completion prefix is used, consistently with c_0=1 and all later c_j=0.
Adding z above D and incomparable with N is a consistent poset construction because N has no predecessor relation into D. Rank |D|+j+1 forces exactly D and N_j before z; the remaining chain has a unique order. Thus its rank count is a_j exactly, with no binomial factor. The cited rank inequality applies at every requested interior index. Monotonicity follows separately from appending n_j. No log-concavity of c_j follows or is used.

3. Backend first-two-step matrix and its 4 by 4 compression
In Q_j the minima are u,y and, when j<R, n_(j+1). Every other point outside the neutral suffix is above an entry, so deletion of n_(j+1) alone cannot release such a point. Its newly released successor is only n_(j+2), when present. Therefore the actual minimal-element first-two-step matrix is
  B_j = [[V_j, C_(j+1)], [C_(j+1)^T, E_(j+2)]].
For j=R the last coordinate is a zero padding.
To derive its one-positive-direction property from the cited CP input, adjoin an isolated marker a to Q_j, use k=2 in the CP construction, and take the down-copy minimal-element principal submatrix. The marker is fixed at rank 3. Distinct-coordinate entries count e(Q_j-z-w); the diagonal counts a first entry z followed by its strict successor, exactly the release diagonal in the draft. This specialization is within the stated k range because Q_j has at least u and y.
The row sums give V_j 1+C_(j+1)=C_j and E_j-E_(j+1)=1^T C_j. In B_j use vectors e_u,e_y,1_all,e_neutral as columns of a compression map. Their pairings give M_j exactly. For j=R take the neutral vector zero. Such a congruence compression cannot increase the positive inertia index. The fourth column is redundant, not an independent extra positive direction.

4. Independent counting justification for W_i
For a residual Q_i, consider an extension with u before y and no strict u-successor before y. Immediately before y the extension consists of j-i neutral points and u for a unique j>=i. The entry u can occupy any of the j-i+1 gaps of that neutral prefix, and the tail has e(Q_j-u-y) completions. This gives the off-diagonal shared count sum_(j>=i)(j-i+1)(V_j)_uy, equally for the reverse orientation.
For an extension with a strict u-successor before y, let v be the first such successor. Before v there are only u and j-i neutral points. Removing these neutral points and u makes v minimal. Conversely, every v in min(Q_j-u) strictly above u, every tail extension of Q_j-u-v, and each of the j-i+1 positions of u yields exactly one such extension. This proves the release diagonal formula with the same coefficient. The reverse orientation is identical. There is no unweighted deletion projection in this argument.
Partitioning by the precise ordered prefix which completes D yields W=sum_i c_i W_i, hence coefficient sum_(i<=j)(j-i+1)c_i=f_j. Telescoping V_j1=C_j-C_(j+1) gives W1=sum_j a_j C_j. The total count is sum_i c_i E_i. Thus (A)/(B), including their original-law interpretation, are verified independently of the finite calibration.

5. Orthogonality and local quadratic expansion
Write x_j=h^T C_j. The tail definition of g gives x_j=E_(j+1)g_(j+1)-E_jg_j. Summing a_j x_j yields -sum_j c_j E_jg_j; consequently h^Ts=0 is exactly (C). In particular g_0 cannot be suppressed in general.
For j<R put v=(h,g_(j+1),-g_(j+1)) in M_j and let b denote the final scalar basis vector. Direct expansion gives
  v^T M_j b = E_(j+2)(g_(j+2)-g_(j+1)),
and
  v^T M_j v - E_(j+2)(g_(j+2)-g_(j+1))^2
  = h^T V_j h + 2A_(j+1)-A_j-A_(j+2)
      + E_j(g_j-g_(j+1))^2.
For j<=R-2, projection along the positive b-direction makes the left side nonpositive. This proves (D) without any g_0=0 assumption.
For j=R-1, b is isotropic but M_j b=(C_R,E_R,0)^T is nonzero and v^T M_j b=0. The previous scalar direction a satisfies a^T M_j a=E_(R-1)>0 and a^T M_j b=E_R>0. Projection onto b+epsilon a gives the exact bound stated in the draft, which tends to zero. Thus the same expansion proves (D) at this endpoint with no division by zero.
For j=R, projection onto the all-minima direction of V_R gives h^T V_Rh <= x_R^2/E_R=A_R. This is the required separate last bound. It also covers R=0; in that case orthogonality forces g_0=0. The R=1 case uses only the isotropic endpoint and this last bound, so no hidden minimum-length restriction remains.

6. Weighted summation and probability normalization
In the weighted sum of 2A_(j+1)-A_j-A_(j+2), with the separate final -f_R A_R, the coefficient of every A_k is
  -f_k+2f_(k-1)-f_(k-2)=-c_k.
Both lower-index coefficients are zero by convention; the upper A_(R+1), A_(R+2) vanish. This proves (E) with the stated negative variance term and positive energy weights.
For h=(1-p,-p), the tail sum of the u coordinate of C is E_j q_j and the sum of both coordinates is E_j. Hence g_j=p-q_j, and h^Ts=0. Expanding h^TWh using W1=s proves -h^TWh/E=p^2-U. The normalization sum c_j E_j=E confirms that c_j E_j/E are probabilities. Thus (F) is correct and is an energy-minus-variance lower bound, not an established nonnegative lower bound.

8. Certification ceiling and concrete remaining gap
Supported: the exact prefix/backend interfaces, Stanley log-concavity of the real a_j, the CP-based local blocks and weighted energy lower bound, plus the explicit falsity of the universal scalar statement (G).
Not supported: a nonnegative right side in (E)/(F) for all actual backend directions, the determinant inequality for shared nonempty D, or a realizable counterexample to that determinant inequality. An actual direction has g_j=-(sum_(l>=j)h^T C_l)/E_j for the SAME h and SAME backend and satisfies the weighted orthogonality. Replacing this correlated family by arbitrary mean-zero scalar g is precisely the invalid closure. In the supplied symmetric counterexample the actual direction is zero, so it cannot refute the determinant target.
The remaining task is to prove compensation for these realizable correlated directions or retain additional matrix information in a stronger gluing argument. The present lower bound could also be too weak even for actual directions; the draft does not establish its sufficiency.
