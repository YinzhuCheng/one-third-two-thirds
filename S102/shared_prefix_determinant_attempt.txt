The external inputs used below are rank log-concavity and the one-positive-direction property of the correct first-two-step matrix. The remaining reductions are derived here. No claim of originality or comprehensive literature search.

1. Scope and the exact target

Let Q be a finite poset, u || y, with identical strict downsets D. All counts use complete uniform linear extensions of Q, never the unweighted deletion projection. Let U be the probability that some strict successor of u occurs before y, and p=Pr(u<y).
The checked fiber decomposition gives W=[[Ru,H],[H,Ry]], E=e(Q)=2H+Ru+Ry, p=(H+Ru)/E, U=Ru/E. Thus
  p^2-U=(H^2-Ru Ry)/E^2.
The target is H^2 >= Ru Ry.

For the remainder assume that
  N=Q \ (D union upward(u) union upward(y))
is a chain n1<...<nR, with upward sets including their generators. This assumption holds whenever width(Q)<=3: each point of N is incomparable with both u and y, so two incomparable points of N would form a four-point antichain with u,y. This reduction allows D and the entire successor backend to be arbitrary, subject to Q being a poset.

No point of N can lie below a point of D, because then it would be below u and belong to D. No point above u or y can precede a point of N, because then that N-point would belong to upward(u) or upward(y). Consequently D union N_j is an ideal for every N_j={n1,...,nj}.

2. Real prefix counts and the additional log-concavity

Define
  a_j=e(Q[D union N_j]), 0<=j<=R,
  a_{-1}=0,
  c_j=a_j-a_{j-1},
  f_j=sum_{i=0}^j a_i=sum_{i=0}^j (j-i+1)c_i.
For D empty, interpret a_j=1, c_0=1, c_j=0 for j>0.
For D nonempty, c_j counts the ordered prefixes on D union N_j whose last entry belongs to D. Indeed, the prefixes ending in N_j are in bijection with all extensions of D union N_{j-1}, by appending N_j. All other prefixes end in D. These are exactly the prefixes at the instant the last member of D has appeared, with precisely j members of N already consumed.
In particular, c_j>=0. These counts depend only on the induced subposet D union N, not on how later successors are ordered. The choice of N itself, of course, is determined by Q.

There is also a rigorous rank realization of a_j. Form K from Q[D union N] by adjoining a new marker z above every element of D and incomparable with every element of N. In a linear extension with z at rank |D|+j+1, the prefix is precisely a linear extension of D union N_j, and the suffix is the unique order on the remaining chain N. Therefore
  #{L in LE(K): rank_L(z)=|D|+j+1}=a_j.
Stanley's rank inequality yields
  a_j^2 >= a_{j-1}a_{j+1}  (1<=j<R).
Thus the prefix weights are not arbitrary: a_j is nondecreasing and log-concave. This does NOT assert log-concavity of its differences c_j, and no such assertion is needed for the valid reductions here.

3. Correct backend matrices and the exact weighted sum

Put Q_j=Q-(D union N_j), E_j=e(Q_j), and C_j=(e(Q_j-u),e(Q_j-y))^T. In Q_j the points u,y are minimal, and its avoid-entry set is exactly the suffix chain N\N_j. Use the S98 matrix
  (V_j)_{uy}=(V_j)_{yu}=e(Q_j-u-y),
  (V_j)_{zz}=sum_{v in min(Q_j-z), z<v} e(Q_j-z-v), z in {u,y}.
These are actual release counts; the diagonal is not a fictitious repeated deletion.
Set E_{R+1}=E_{R+2}=0, C_{R+1}=0. The real first-two-step matrix implies that
  M_j = [[V_j, C_j, C_{j+1}],
         [C_j^T, E_j, E_{j+1}],
         [C_{j+1}^T, E_{j+1}, E_{j+2}]]
has at most one positive eigenvalue, for j=0,...,R. Also
  V_j 1=C_j-C_{j+1}.

Conditional on one of the c_i specified ordered prefixes completing D at N_i, the remaining extension is uniform on Q_i. S98 gives its full shared/private matrix as
  W_i=sum_{j=i}^R (j-i+1)V_j.
Hence the ORIGINAL Q has
  W=sum_{i=0}^R c_i W_i=sum_{j=0}^R f_j V_j.                 (A)
Moreover
  s=W1=sum_{j=0}^R a_j C_j,
  E=1^T W1=sum_{i=0}^R c_i E_i.                            (B)
The u-coordinate of s counts extensions with u<y. It also follows directly by choosing j neutral points before the first of u,y: the prefix contributes a_j, followed by the chosen entry and its C_j completion count.

Equation (A) is a new, exact interface for the shared-downset problem under the neutral-chain assumption. It does not prove one-positive-direction preservation: the coefficients f_j differ from the linear coefficients j+1 in S98, and a positive sum of hyperbolic matrices is not generally hyperbolic.

4. Weighted S98 elimination and its remaining quadratic gap

Let h be any real two-vector with h^T s=0. Write x_j=h^T C_j and define
  g_j=-(sum_{l=j}^R x_l)/E_j for 0<=j<=R,
  g_{R+1}=g_{R+2}=0,
  A_j=E_j g_j^2.
Then
  x_j=E_{j+1}g_{j+1}-E_jg_j,
  sum_{j=0}^R c_j E_j g_j=0.                               (C)
The second identity follows by summation by parts from h^Ts=sum a_j x_j=0. Notice that g_0 need not be zero. Setting g_0=0 would silently revert to a different orthogonality constraint.

The local S98 calculation, with no use of g_0=0, gives
  -h^T V_j h >= 2A_{j+1}-A_j-A_{j+2}
                  +E_j(g_j-g_{j+1})^2,                    (D)
for 0<=j<R. For j=R the available endpoint bound is
  -h^T V_R h >= -A_R.
For j<=R-2, (D) is exactly the reverse-projection version of S98's local inequality. There is a harmless isotropic endpoint extension at j=R-1, explained below; it is not obtained by dividing by E_{R+1}=0.

Multiply (D) by f_j and sum, including the last endpoint. Since
  f_j-2f_{j-1}+f_{j-2}=c_j,  f_{-1}=f_{-2}=0,
one obtains the rigorous bound
  -h^T W h >=
    sum_{j=0}^{R-1} f_j E_j(g_j-g_{j+1})^2
      -sum_{j=0}^R c_j E_j g_j^2.                         (E)

Isotropic endpoint detail: use v=(h,g_R,-g_R) in M_{R-1} and b equal to its final coordinate. Then b^T M b=0, M b=(C_R,E_R,0)^T is nonzero, and v^T M b=x_R+E_R g_R=0. Take the previous scalar coordinate a, so a^T M a=E_{R-1}>0 and a^T M b=E_R>0. Applying the one-positive-direction inequality to b+epsilon a gives
  v^T M v <= epsilon^2(v^T M a)^2/(2epsilon E_R+epsilon^2 E_{R-1}).
Let epsilon decrease to zero. Thus v^T M v<=0, and the same expansion as for the positive reverse-projection case gives (D). No inverse zero denominator is used.

For h=(1-p,-p), let q_j=Pr_{Q_j}(u<y). The definitions give g_j=p-q_j. Also
  -h^TWh/E=p^2-U.
Consequently (E) becomes
  p^2-U >= (1/E)sum_{j=0}^{R-1} f_j E_j(q_j-q_{j+1})^2
                -(1/E)sum_{j=0}^R c_j E_j(q_j-p)^2.        (F)
The weights c_j E_j/E are precisely the true completion-prefix probabilities. The last term is their direction variance. Equation (F) makes explicit what the weighted energy would need to compensate; it does not assert that compensation occurs.

5. A precise failure of a tempting scalar closure

A sufficient next lemma would have been the following weighted Poincare statement for EVERY vector g satisfying (C):
  sum c_j E_j g_j^2 <= sum f_j E_j(g_j-g_{j+1})^2.           (G)
Even with real poset weights and log-concave a_j, (G) is false.

Take Q consisting of D={d}, u,y with d<u and d<y, and an independent eight-point chain N. There are no other relations. Its width is 3. Here R=8 and, for j=0,...,8,
  a_j=j+1, c_j=1, f_j=(j+1)(j+2)/2,
  E_j=(10-j)(9-j).
The a_j are exactly the genuine prefix counts and are log-concave. Set
  mu=(sum_{j=0}^8 E_j/(j+1))/(sum_{j=0}^8 E_j),
  g_j=1/(j+1)-mu.
Then sum c_j E_jg_j=0, but exact rational arithmetic gives
  sum c_j E_jg_j^2 = 359035429/10478160,
  sum_{j=0}^7 f_j E_j(g_j-g_{j+1})^2 = 27527/840,
  difference = 15663631/10478160 > 0.
The script shared_prefix_determinant_check.py reproduces these numbers.

THIS IS NOT A COUNTEREXAMPLE TO THE TARGET. In this symmetric Q, q_j=1/2 for every j, and the genuine direction vector h forces g_j=0. The counterexample vector above cannot arise from the C_j and h of that Q. Its purpose is to locate the missing interface sharply: a proof cannot discard the correlation between C_j and E_j and use only the scalar realizability/log-concavity of the prefix weights plus a universal mean-zero Poincare claim.

6. What is established, and what remains

Established:
- The common-downset, width<=3 problem has a single neutral-chain spine.
- Its true prefix multiplicities a_j are rank counts, hence log-concave.
- Its exact original-law release matrix is W=sum f_j V_j with f_j=sum_{i<=j}a_i.
- The S98 shared-block argument gives the valid energy-minus-variance lower bound (E)/(F), including a rigorously handled isotropic endpoint.
- A natural scalar sufficient closure (G) is false on weights from an explicit width-3 poset.

Not established:
- H^2 >= Ru Ry, even under width<=3 and shared D.
- A realizable counterexample to that determinant inequality.
- A proof that log-concavity of a_j plus the full backend CP blocks suffices.

The remaining usable target is a constrained inequality for the ACTUAL sequence g_j=-(sum_{l>=j}h^TC_l)/E_j, with the C_j and E_j from one backend and h^T sum a_jC_j=0. Alternatively, a stronger matrix gluing argument must retain information lost by the scalar projection in (E). This work does not solve MAIN33, WIDTH3, MC3, or the S99 normalized square candidate.

7. Independent finite calibration (not a proof)

The same script also enumerates every permutation of the asymmetric seven-point poset
  d<u, d<y, u<s, y<t, n1<n2, n1<s, n2<t
and its transitive closure. With D={d}, N=(n1,n2), it finds
  a=(1,2,3), c=(1,1,1), f=(1,3,6),
  (E_0,E_1,E_2)=(40,20,6),
  E=66, W=[[12,24],[24,6]].
The direct release-event counts agree exactly with sum f_j V_j. Furthermore
  p=6/11, U=2/11, p^2-U=14/121,
while the energy-minus-variance right side in (F) is 1/484.
This checks the nontrivial index conventions and original-law weighting on one asymmetric instance. It does not establish the missing universal sign.
