# OC1: an exact common-successor core when k=m+1

2026-11-10. **Analytic structural candidate, independent review requested.** No new probability inequality is asserted except entry into an existing dual-VG guard in the zero-exception case.

## Contract

Use the original exact B1 lift, actual full Delta10, k=m+1, and zero x-containing C-minima. Suppose f is an empty-signature C-minimum outside E. The current m6k7 and m5k6 strong residuals supply this premise. This manuscript takes that premise explicitly and does not reprove the preceding LM1 reductions.

Since E consists of m missing-x points, the cardinality condition forces

    Mx=E disjoint-union {f}.

Put Q=C−Mx=U_C(x). Define the actual exceptional set

    Z={q in Q:q is incomparable with f}, W=Q−Z.

## 1. Exact structure, not independent private-set budgets

Because f is a C-minimum, every q in Q is either above f or incomparable with f. Consequently W=U_C(f). The full strict lower and upper sets of x,f satisfy

    D(x)={a,u}, D(f)={a,b,u},
    U(x)=Q=W disjoint-union Z, U(f)=W.

No additional outside-C successor is omitted: x is incomparable with the other old roots and b, and f is above a,b,u but below no T root.

Every E point is incomparable with f. The direction E<f contradicts C-minimality; the direction f<E contradicts E being an ideal. Therefore

    deg_(P+)(f)=3+m+|Z|,

where the three outside-C incomparable labels are x,y,z. Actual Delta10 gives |Z|≤7−m. This is an equality for the full degree, not merely a partial neighbor count.

Z is an ideal in Q: if q'<q in Q and q is incomparable with f, then f<q' would imply f<q; the reverse q'<f is impossible by minimality of f. Thus q' belongs to Z. Equivalently W is a Q filter, and every member of W is above both f and x.

Every Z-minimum has a nonempty E predecessor set. Otherwise it would have no C predecessor: no point of Q can precede it outside Z, no other Z point precedes it by minimality, and f cannot precede it. It would then be an x-containing C-minimum, contrary to zero frontier. All such predecessors are in E. In particular every Z-minimum inherits y,z as well as x, hence has full signature. Every Z point lies above a Z-minimum, so **all of Z has full signature**.

Moreover E union Z is a genuine C ideal. A predecessor of a Z point can only lie in E or Z; f and W are excluded. E is already an ideal. Thus the actual remainder consists of two low-level pieces, the ideal E union Z and the separate minimum f, with W above f,x and arbitrary order-consistent relationships to E,Z. No arbitrary weighted replacement of W is licensed.

## 2. Consequences in the two strong cells

For m6k7, |Z|≤1. If Z is nonempty, it is a single full label g. Its entire C predecessor set is a nonempty E ideal A=D_C(g). Every other x-containing label lies above f and x. The only private upper successor of x versus f is g; the only private lower predecessor of f versus x is b. Importantly b<g in the original order. These facts identify the exact one-exception configuration but do not establish a balance inequality for x,f.

For m5k6, |Z|≤2. If nonempty, Z is either a singleton full label or two full labels, comparable or incomparable. Every Z-minimum has a nonempty E predecessor ideal. For a nonminimal point in a two-chain Z, predecessors in C lie in E plus the preceding Z label. This finite exception structure is forced even if W is arbitrarily large and nonautonomous.

If Z is empty in either cell, x,f have exactly the same full strict upper set W. Their private strict lower differences are empty and {b}, both chains. Thus they constitute an **original dual-VG configuration**; the accepted dual old guard gives ordinary balance. It does not necessarily identify x,f themselves as the balanced pair. Accordingly a residual avoiding all original dual-VG guards must have

    m6k7: |Z|=1;   m5k6: 1≤|Z|≤2.

This is a necessary structural conclusion, not a realization assertion or new ordinary-population mechanism.

## 3. The gap that remains

When |Z|=1, the two private sets lie at different endpoints: D(f)−D(x)={b}, while U(x)−U(f)={g}. Their union has two labels, not one. The statement “each endpoint has one spare neighbor” cannot reduce their joint size or invoke a product≤2 certificate. Also D(x) and D(f) differ, and U(x) and U(f) differ, so x,f are not automatically positive or dual VG.

The common-successor set W retains all actual relations and completion weights. A selected g may have only a small nonempty E ideal below it, while other E points and W can interleave. This manuscript does not replace those true weights with a uniform exceptional-label shuffle or claim an inherited moment. A further payment proof must use this exact core plus the true E/W extension law, or exhibit another original guard after checking its full lower/upper sets.

There is no original-poset enumeration, computer-assisted positivity certificate, or guard-excluded control in this note. The new result is the exact finite exceptional ideal and the exhaustion of the zero-exception case, outside the previously closed uniform-Q architecture.
