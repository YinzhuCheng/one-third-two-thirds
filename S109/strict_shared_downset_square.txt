S109: Strict shared-downset swap square for every finite poset
2026-10-10. Proof statement; independent audit status is recorded in the companion review.
Depends on the non-strict S108 Ball-body proof. No additional analytic equality theorem is used.
No frozen/published snapshot is edited; no novelty claim is made.

THEOREM
Let P be a finite poset, and let u,y be incomparable elements with identical
strict downsets. Let H count the linear extensions with u before y for which
swapping the actual labels u and y is still a linear extension. Let Ru,Ry count
the nonswappable extensions in the forward and reverse orientations.
Then
                              H^2 > Ru Ry.
In particular, equality never occurs for a finite poset under these hypotheses.

The extra structure beyond S108 is elementary: on each deletion-extension
simplex, the two fiber widths are nested sums of uniform-simplex spacings.
An associated radial moment is therefore a polynomial of the direction ratio
on (0,1). Equality in S108 would make that same moment the reciprocal square
of a nonconstant linear polynomial, which is impossible.

1. NOTATION INHERITED FROM S108
Set n=|P| and m=n-2. Let Omega=O(P-{u,y}), and define L,Tu,Ty,A=Tu-L,
B=Ty-L and the decreasing log-concave fiber function f exactly as in S108:
  f(s,t)=vol_m{z in Omega: A(z)>=s, B(z)>=t}.
Thus
  h=H/n!=integral_Omega min(A,B)^2/2 dz,
  rplus=Ru/n!, rminus=Ry/n!.
For 0<q<1 define
  I(q)=integral_0^infinity r f(r,qr) dr
      =integral_Omega min(A,B/q)^2/2 dz.                    (1)
The second equality is Tonelli applied to the defining superlevel indicators.
The Ball body K from S108 satisfies, for x=(1,q),
  max{t>=0: tx in K}^2 = 2 I(q).                         (2)
This follows either from the norm definition or from its unit-direction radial
formula by rescaling r. We have I(q)>0 and h>0 because A,B are positive on the
interior of the full-dimensional deletion order polytope. The m=0 convention
also gives I(q)=h=1/2.

2. POLYNOMIAL RAY-MOMENT LEMMA
Claim: I(q) agrees with a real polynomial of degree at most m for all 0<q<1.

Partition Omega, up to measure-zero coordinate ties, into its standard
simplices indexed by the linear extensions of Q=P-{u,y}. In one such simplex,
rename coordinates in extension order:
  0=z_0<z_1<...<z_m<z_(m+1)=1.
The simplex has volume 1/m!. There are fixed indices ell,i,j such that
  L=z_ell, Tu=z_i, Ty=z_j,
where ell=0 if the common downset is empty and i or j equals m+1 if the
corresponding successor set is empty. Otherwise each index is the last common
predecessor, or the first respective successor, in this fixed extension.
All common predecessors precede all successors of either missing element, so
  0<=ell<min(i,j)<=m+1.
Consequently A=z_i-z_ell and B=z_j-z_ell.

If i<=j, then A<=B throughout the simplex. Since 0<q<1, the integrand in (1)
is A^2/2, independent of q, so the chamber contribution is constant.

Suppose i>j. Put
  a=j-ell>=1, b=i-j>=1, k=a+b=i-ell, c=m+1-k>=0.
Under normalized uniform measure on this standard simplex, its m+1 spacings
  z_1-z_0, z_2-z_1, ..., z_(m+1)-z_m
are uniform on the simplex of nonnegative spacings summing to 1. Group them
into B (a spacings), A-B (b spacings), and 1-A (c spacings).
If c>0, their joint density is a constant times
  B^(a-1) (A-B)^(b-1) (1-A)^(c-1),
on 0<B<A<1. This follows by the volumes of the three component simplices.
Under the change of variables B=AV, the Jacobian is A, so the density factors
as a function of A times
  V^(a-1)(1-V)^(b-1),  0<V<1.
Thus V=B/A is independent of A and has beta density
  V^(a-1)(1-V)^(b-1)/Beta(a,b).
If c=0, A=1 identically and the same beta law for V follows by the two-group
simplex calculation. Therefore the independence statement is still valid.

It follows that this chamber's contribution to (1) is
  E[A^2]/(2m!) * G_(a,b)(q),
where
  G_(a,b)(q)=E[min(1,(V/q)^2)]
    =1 - 1/Beta(a,b) * integral_0^q
                (1-v^2/q^2) v^(a-1)(1-v)^(b-1) dv
    =1 - 2/Beta(a,b) * sum_(r=0)^(b-1)
       [(-1)^r binom(b-1,r) q^(a+r)/((a+r)(a+r+2))].       (3)
The last equality is just the finite binomial expansion of (1-v)^(b-1).
This is a polynomial in q of degree a+b-1=k-1<=m.
For reference, E[A^2]=k(k+1)/((m+1)(m+2)), including the c=0 endpoint;
this numerical value is not needed for polynomiality.
There are finitely many chambers, so summing their polynomial contributions
proves the claim. For m=0 there is only the constant contribution.

3. EQUALITY IN THE SUPPORTING-TRIANGLE STEP
Assume, for contradiction, H^2=RuRy. Equivalently h^2=rplus*rminus.
Because h>0, both wings rplus,rminus are positive.
Let (d,d) be K's positive diagonal boundary point, so h=d^2/2. A support line
there has equation
  alpha*s+beta*t=(alpha+beta)d,
with alpha,beta>=0. In S108, alpha=0 forces rplus=0, and beta=0 forces
rminus=0. Hence here alpha,beta>0.
The first-quadrant portion of K is contained in the triangle
  T={(s,t)>=0: alpha*s+beta*t <= (alpha+beta)d}.
The S108 wing estimates are
  rplus <= alpha*d^2/(2beta),
  rminus <= beta*d^2/(2alpha).
Their upper bounds have product h^2. Since both quantities are nonnegative
and their product equals h^2>0, both inequalities must be equalities.
Thus K and T have equal area separately in both diagonal sectors, and hence
in the whole quadrant. Closedness and convexity imply K intersect quadrant=T:
otherwise a missing point of T, using an interior point of T if necessary,
would give a positive-area subset of T outside the closed set K.
In particular, for every 0<q<1 the endpoint of the ray (t,qt) is
  t=(alpha+beta)d/(alpha+beta*q).
Equation (2) gives
  I(q)=((alpha+beta)^2*d^2)/(2*(alpha+beta*q)^2).            (4)

4. FINITE-POLYNOMIAL CONTRADICTION
By Section 2 there is a nonzero polynomial P(q) with I(q)=P(q) on (0,1).
Equation (4) says
  (alpha+beta*q)^2 P(q)=((alpha+beta)^2*d^2)/2
on that nonempty open interval. Thus it is an identity of polynomials.
But beta>0 and P is nonzero, so the left side has degree deg(P)+2>=2,
whereas the right side is a positive constant. This is impossible.
Therefore equality cannot hold. S108 supplies the non-strict inequality,
so H^2>RuRy, as asserted. QED.

5. SCOPE AND CAUTIONS
- This argument does not assume that the Ball body of every compactly
  supported log-concave function is strictly convex. That assertion is false
  for indicators of convex bodies with faces.
- It does not rely only on the square support of the particular f. The
  finite nested-spacing polynomial is the extra proved obstruction.
- General decreasing log-concave functions can attain equality (for example,
  appropriate exponentials or indicators of triangles). Their directional
  moment is reciprocal-square rather than a finite polynomial on this wedge.
- The result is qualitative strictness. No new quantitative coefficient or
  uniform margin beyond strict positivity is claimed here.
- The theorem does not close any global one-third/two-thirds reduction.
