STATEMENT VERIFIED
For every finite poset P and incomparable u,y with the same strict downset,
let H be the number of forward extensions preserved by swapping the actual
labels u,y, and Ru,Ry the respective nonswappable counts. Then
                         H^2 > Ru Ry.
The strictness deduction is sound for the original complete-extension counts;
no change to a uniform law on deletion extensions occurs.

1. DELETION-CHAMBER INDICES
Write m=n-2. A linear-extension chamber of the deletion order polytope is
  0=z_0<z_1<...<z_m<z_(m+1)=1,
with volume 1/m!. On it the three endpoint functions are fixed coordinates:
L=z_l, Tu=z_i, Ty=z_j. Each common predecessor d is below every successor v
of u or y, since d<u<v or d<y<v. Consequently every such predecessor appears
before every such successor in every deletion extension. Thus
  0 <= l < min(i,j) <= m+1.
This is the exact condition needed to have positive integer spacing counts.
Empty D gives l=0. A missing successor set gives its upper index m+1.
The widths A=z_i-z_l and B=z_j-z_l share their lower endpoint, so their
spacing blocks are nested. Arbitrary mixtures of unrelated rectangles would
not provide this property; the proof does not assert they do.

2. INDEPENDENT DERIVATION OF THE DIRECTIONAL MOMENT
For 0<q<1, Tonelli gives
  J(q)=integral_0^infinity r f(r,qr) dr
      =(1/2) integral_Omega min(A,B/q)^2 dz.
For one deletion chamber sigma, distinguish the two index orders.

If i<=j, then A<=B and min(A,B/q)=A. Put k=i-l>=1. The chamber contributes
  J_sigma(q)= k(k+1)/(2 n!),
a positive constant.

If i>j, put a=j-l>=1, b=i-j>=1, k=a+b=i-l, and c=m+1-k>=0.
The m+1 unit-total spacings have the uniform Dirichlet(1,...,1) law under
normalized chamber measure. Group them into
  B: a spacings;
  A-B: b spacings;
  1-A: c spacings.
The final group includes the l initial spacings AND the m+1-i terminal
spacings. These need not be contiguous; the simplex-volume grouping formula
only uses the size of each group, so noncontiguity causes no restriction.

For c>0, the joint (B,A) density is proportional to
  B^(a-1)(A-B)^(b-1)(1-A)^(c-1),   0<B<A<1.
Put V=B/A. The Jacobian of (A,V)->(A,B) is A, giving density proportional to
  A^(a+b-1)(1-A)^(c-1) V^(a-1)(1-V)^(b-1).
Hence A and V are independent, V~Beta(a,b), and
  E[A^2]=k(k+1)/((m+1)(m+2)).
For c=0, necessarily l=0 and i=m+1, so A=1 identically. The remaining
2-group simplex calculation still gives V~Beta(a,b), and the displayed
second-moment formula equals 1. Independence with a constant is valid.

Thus, using n!=m!(m+1)(m+2),
  J_sigma(q) = k(k+1)/(2 n!) G_(a,b)(q),
where
  G_(a,b)(q)
   = q^(-2) E[V^2 1_(V<q)] + Pr(V>=q)
   = 1 - (1/Beta(a,b)) integral_0^q
                   (1-v^2/q^2) v^(a-1)(1-v)^(b-1) dv
   = 1 - (2/Beta(a,b)) sum_(r=0)^(b-1)
       (-1)^r binom(b-1,r) q^(a+r)/[(a+r)(a+r+2)].
The coefficient calculation uses
  q^(-2) integral_0^q v^(a+r+1)dv
       - integral_0^q v^(a+r-1)dv
  = -2 q^(a+r)/[(a+r)(a+r+2)].
No denominator vanishes because a+r>=1. The highest possible degree is
  a+b-1 = k-1 <= m.
Summing finitely many chambers gives a real polynomial J on (0,1).
Every chamber weight is its actual Euclidean volume 1/m!, retained throughout.

3. ENDPOINT AND DEGENERATE-CASE AUDIT
- Empty common downset: l=0; all calculations remain valid.
- Ty=1, hence j=m+1: necessarily i<=j, so the chamber is in the constant
  branch. No Beta distribution with an invalid parameter is invoked.
- Tu=1, hence i=m+1: either i=j and the contribution is constant, or i>j
  and c=l. If also l=0, this is exactly the separately checked A=1 case.
- Tu=Ty on a chamber: i=j, hence A=B. The constant branch applies. The
  proof never invokes Beta(a,0); its Beta formula is restricted to b>=1.
- Both successor sets empty: A=B=1-L. Again the constant branch applies.
- D nonempty: l can be as large as m but then the upper index is m+1;
  still k>=1, as required.
- m=0, n=2: P consists of the incomparable pair. The 0-dimensional
  deletion-polytope measure is 1, A=B=1, J(q)=1/2, H=1, Ru=Ry=0.
- q=0 and q=1 are not used by the polynomial identity argument; it uses
  the open interval (0,1). No division by zero or boundary regularization
  is hidden. The polynomial extends continuously to these endpoints if
  desired; J(1)=H/n! and J(0)=(1/2) integral A^2.
- If either Ru or Ry vanishes, strictness is immediate from H>0.
  For m>0, positive widths in the deletion-polytope interior give H>0.
  Combinatorially, take a linear extension of the common downset D,
  then u,y consecutively, then extend the rest. Swapping this consecutive
  pair is legal, so H>=1. This also covers all dimensions directly.

4. BALL-BODY NORMALIZATION CHECK
The S108 rescaled Ball norm is
  N_K(x) = [2 integral_0^infinity r F(rx)dr]^(-1/2).
For x=(1,q), integral rF(rx)dr=J(q). Homogeneity therefore gives
  max{t>=0: t(1,q) in K} = sqrt(2J(q)).
This is a ray parameter, not Euclidean distance; the draft correctly uses
this convention and introduces no missing factor 1+q^2.
At the diagonal boundary point (d,d), d^2=2h and h=H/n!.
The count normalization and the factor 2 in the ray identity both agree
with S108 exactly.

5. EQUALITY FORCES A FULL NONDEGENERATE TRIANGLE
Assume equality h^2=rplus*rminus. Since h>0, both wings are positive.
A supporting line of unconditional convex K at (d,d) is
  alpha*s+beta*t=(alpha+beta)d,  alpha,beta>=0.
The zero-coefficient cases force a zero wing, as in S108. Thus equality
requires alpha,beta>0. With T the first-quadrant triangle below this line,
K intersect quadrant is contained in T. The two wing estimates are
  rplus <= alpha*d^2/(2beta),
  rminus <= beta*d^2/(2alpha).
Their positive upper bounds have product h^2. Equality of the product
therefore forces equality in each estimate separately. Subtracting or
adding the same core half-square area h preserves the area equality, so
K and T have equal area in both sectors and hence in the whole quadrant.

The conclusion K intersect quadrant=T is justified. If a point of compact
T lay outside closed K, its distance to K would be positive. Since T is
full-dimensional, a nearby interior point of T would also lie outside K,
and a sufficiently small open disk around that point would give positive
missing area, contradicting equality. Thus boundary-only omissions cannot
invalidate the conclusion. No stronger strict-convexity theorem is used.

For every q in (0,1), the ray parameter in this triangle is
  t=(alpha+beta)d/(alpha+beta*q).
The normalization checked above then forces
  J(q)=((alpha+beta)^2 d^2)/(2(alpha+beta*q)^2).

6. POLYNOMIAL CONTRADICTION
The polynomial P with J(q)=P(q) is nonzero because J(q)>0. Equality would
give, on a nonempty open interval,
  (alpha+beta*q)^2 P(q)=((alpha+beta)^2 d^2)/2.
Hence this is a polynomial identity. The left side has degree deg(P)+2
because beta>0 and P is nonzero; the right side is a positive constant.
This is impossible. Together with the audited non-strict inequality,
it proves strictness in every finite case.

7. EXACT SANITY CHECKS (SUPPLEMENTARY, NOT THE PROOF)
For two disjoint 2-element chains u<a and y<b, n=4 and the deletion poset
on a,b is an antichain. One chamber contributes 1/24. The other contributes
(1/8)(1-2q/3). Thus J(q)=1/6-q/12 and J(1)=1/12.
The six complete extensions give H=2, Ru=Ry=1, so H/n!=1/12 and
H^2-RuRy=3>0. This checks the count normalization and nonconstant case.
The n=2 antichain gives J=1/2=H/2!.
Additionally, exact symbolic integration verified the stated finite-sum
identity for all 64 pairs a,b in {1,...,8}; no floating-point sampling was
used. The general algebraic derivation in Section 2 is the justification.
