S109 — Targeted prior-art check: shared-downset actual-swap square
Checked 2026-10-10 UTC. Research comparison, not a novelty certificate.

RESULT

No exact published statement of the full shared-downset actual-label-swap
inequality was identified in this targeted check. This is NOT evidence of
novelty and does not establish that the statement is absent from the literature.

Two concrete prior-art relationships were established:

1. Chan–Pak's 2024 Theorem 1.9 yields the exact square in the special case
   with no element incomparable to both distinguished elements (N empty).
   A direct application of that theorem to arbitrary N was not established.
2. The route from poset probabilities through convex bodies/log-concavity and
   Ball's theorem to a two-dimensional geometric problem predates this work:
   Kahn–Yu (1998) explicitly used that route. It must not be presented as a
   new general method. Their publicly available abstract describes a different
   target, proportional transitivity. Their complete paper was not obtained,
   so an exact implication through an internal lemma of that paper remains open.

Ball's theorem itself is a verified, sufficiently strong analytic input for
S108, together with the explicit elementary wing argument and the verified
order-polytope event translation. This establishes a derivation from classical
theory; it is distinct from finding the final combinatorial inequality already
stated in print.

TARGET AND NORMALIZATION

The supplied target is: P finite, u incomparable to y, D_P(u)=D_P(y).
H counts full linear extensions with u before y such that interchanging the
two actual labels still gives a linear extension. Ru and Ry count the
nonswappable extensions in the two respective orientations. With E=e(P),

  E=2H+Ru+Ry,   p=(H+Ru)/E,
  H^2 >= Ru Ry  <=>  EH >= (H+Ru)(H+Ry)
                  <=>  H/E >= p(1-p).

The event uses arbitrary positions and a possibly nonempty block between u
and y. It is not simply adjacency, the first two positions, or deletion of
elements followed by a uniform law on the smaller poset.

PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISONS

A. Chan–Pak: the exact N-empty specialization

Swee Hong Chan and Igor Pak, Correlation inequalities for linear extensions,
Advances in Mathematics 458 (2024), article 109954.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2024.109954
Full author version, dated 12 September 2024:
https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/Correlation17.pdf
Relevant locations: Theorem 1.9, p.4, equation (1.17); proof in Section 6.5.

For disjoint nonempty A,B contained in min(P), Theorem 1.9 says

  Pr[1 in f(A),2 in f(B)]^2
  >= Pr[1 in f(A),2 in f(A-up)]
     Pr[1 in f(B),2 in f(B-up)].

These are first-two-rank events. Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 1.7 likewise concern
the first two ranks. Proposition 6.1 gives a hyperbolic matrix with certain
fixed-rank restrictions, not an asserted arbitrary-mixture closure theorem.

The following scope identification is our direct mathematical observation:
assume first D empty and N empty. Then the only minimal elements are u,y.
In a forward extension, if u and y are not the first two elements, the second
element lies above u and before y, preventing the swap. Conversely an extension
starting u,y permits the swap. Thus, for A={u}, B={y}, the left probability is
H/E, and the two right probabilities are Ru/E and Ry/E. This is exactly S108's
square in this special case.

For general common D but still N empty, every remaining element is above at
least one of u,y, and hence above every element of D. Thus P is the ordinal
sum D plus Q, where Q has only u,y minimal. All relevant counts acquire the
same factor e(D); the preceding specialization still applies.

For arbitrary N, the identification fails. For example, for the three-element
antichain {u,y,z}, H=3 while the count of extensions beginning u,y is 1.
Merely summing first-two-rank inequalities over prefixes would need an extra
theorem. In particular, off-diagonal-square dominance is not closed under
arbitrary summation: [[100,10],[10,1]] and [[1,10],[10,100]] each have determinant
zero, while their sum has positive determinant. This observation does not rule
out a more sophisticated deduction from the atlas theory; none was verified.

B. Fishburn: normalized ideal counts and strengthened XYZ

Peter C. Fishburn, A correlational inequality for linear extensions of a poset,
Order 1 (1984), 127–137.
Primary publisher abstract: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00565648
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00565648

The abstract states a sharp bound on
  N(213)N(312)/[(N(123)+N(132))(N(231)+N(321))]
for three pairwise incomparable distinguished points. Its bound is
(n-1)^2/(n+1)^2 for odd n and (n-2)/(n+2) for even n, giving strict XYZ.
That is a six-order-pattern statistic on three points, not the two-point
actual-swap partition. No substitution identifying those counts with H,Ru,Ry
was established.

The associated ideal-count inequality has a fully accessible new proof in:
Graham R. Brightwell and William T. Trotter, A combinatorial approach to
correlation inequalities, Discrete Mathematics 257 (2002), 311–327.
https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/117.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(02)00432-6
Lemma 3.1, p.314: writing epsilon(S)=e(S)/|S|!, for downsets A,B,
  epsilon(A)epsilon(B) <= epsilon(A union B)epsilon(A intersection B).
Theorems 2.1–2.2 discuss XYZ; Theorem 5.1 restates and reproves Shepp's
disjoint-union correlation theorem. These are explicitly different quantities
from H,Ru,Ry. A reduction of the full target to these results was not found.

C. Shepp: arbitrary relations between initially unrelated blocks

L. A. Shepp, The FKG inequality and some monotonicity properties of partial
orders, SIAM Journal on Algebraic and Discrete Methods 1 (1980), 295–299.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/0601034
Full author-hosted original:
https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Fkg-inequality-and-some-monotonicity-properties-of-partial-orders.pdf

Theorem 2, equation (1.6), assumes the original poset has two blocks with no
relations between them and establishes positive association of collections
of added comparisons all pointing from the first block to the second.
Theorem 1 permits pre-existing cross-comparisons when each block is a chain.
The paper explicitly gives counterexamples to removing those restrictions.

Shared downsets alone do not supply such a two-block decomposition: the
successor sets and common-incomparable elements may have cross-relations.
Therefore neither the unrestricted-block theorem nor its two-chain variant
can simply be invoked without constructing and verifying a reduction.

Shepp's 1982 XYZ theorem has DOI https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176993791.
The accessible primary reproof in Brightwell–Trotter above establishes its
usual pair-comparison form. It does not itself identify the swap event.

D. Generalized Fishburn results checked

Chan–Pak, Multivariate correlation inequalities for P-partitions (2023 author
version): https://www.math.ucla.edu/~pak/papers/AlphaBet12.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11954
Theorem 3.4 is a self-dual extension of Fishburn's normalized induced-subposet
count inequality using lower and upper ideals. Theorems 4.9–4.10 extend to
order polynomials and multivariate weights. These keep ideal-deletion/counting
data; no direct encoding of the actual-swap cells was verified.

Chan–Pak, Equality conditions for correlation inequalities (2026):
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06275v1#S4.SS3
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06275
Section 4.3, Theorems 4.4–4.5, states generalized Fishburn and characterizes
equality using connectivity in the comparability graph. This is a recent
primary-source check, but it does not advertise the present swap statistic.

E. Ball: verified analytic implication, not an existing poset statement

Keith Ball, Logarithmically concave functions and sections of convex sets in
R^n, Studia Mathematica 88 (1988), 69–84.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4064/sm-88-1-69-84

The precise form used is verified in Ball's own published survey:
Convex geometry and its connections to harmonic analysis, functional analysis
and probability theory, Proceedings of ICM 2022, vol.4, 3104–3139.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4171/ICM2022/65
Full text: https://ems.press/content/book-chapter-files/33237
Theorem 3, printed p.3108, PDF p.5: for an even nonnegative integrable
log-concave F with positive integral and p>=1,
  x -> [integral_0^infinity F(rx) r^(p-1) dr]^(-1/p)
is a norm. Theorem 2 and adjacent discussion give marginal log-concavity.

S108 uses p=2 and an unconditional extension F(s,t)=f(|s|,|t|). Its further
support-line/sector calculation is explicit, so the classical theorem does
provide a sufficient analytic foundation. Neither the cited theorem statement
nor this inspected survey states H^2>=RuRy for posets. The distinction between
a new derivation here and a new theorem in the literature must remain explicit.

F. Kahn–Yu: important methodological predecessor; full-text comparison open

Jeff Kahn and Yang Yu, Log-Concave Functions And Poset Probabilities,
Combinatorica 18 (1998), 85–99.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/PL00009812
Primary publisher: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/PL00009812
Authors' institution record with readable formulas:
https://www.researchwithrutgers.org/en/publications/log-concave-functions-and-poset-probabilities/

Their abstract defines delta(a,b) as the infimum of Pr(x<z) subject to
Pr(x<y)>=a and Pr(y<z)>=b. It also describes an exact geometric analogue gamma
for a vector uniformly distributed in a compact convex set. It explicitly
identifies Brunn–Minkowski plus Ball as the reduction to two dimensions.
Kahn's 1997 seminar announcement confirms the consequence that probabilities
Pr(x<y)>1/2 and Pr(y<z)>1/2 imply Pr(x<z)>1/4:
https://archive.dimacs.rutgers.edu/archive/Events/1997/Titles/1997/Kahn.html

The advertised target is proportional transitivity of three comparisons.
The present H is a complete swap-validity event involving all relevant
successors, not one of those three comparisons. No verified reduction of H
to the theorem's three-coordinate event was obtained. Importantly, the full
paper may contain geometric lemmas closer to S108 than its abstract; those
lemmas could not be checked here. Publisher access is subscription-only,
and neither the author's selected-paper page nor the queried bibliographic
records supplied an open full text. No subscription, author contact, or
access-control bypass was attempted.

SEARCH COVERAGE AND LIMITS

The check used primary author/publisher copies, inspected relevant theorem
statements and proof setups, and searched wording variants involving identical
downsets/predecessors, actual swaps/interchanges/transpositions, successor
probabilities, and poset applications of Ball. The full Chan–Pak 2024,
Chan–Pak multivariate, Brightwell–Trotter 2002, and Shepp 1980 PDFs were read
at the relevant sections. Recent Chan–Pak 2026 equality conditions were also
checked. Chan–Pak's Linear extensions of finite posets survey was used as a
bibliographic map, not as a substitute for a missing original proof.

As a nearby check, Aires–Kahn, Balancing Extensions in Posets of Large Width,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11549 (v1, 15 September 2025), Sections 3–6,
discusses order polytopes, log-concavity, and XYZ/Fishburn correlations. No
matching actual-swap theorem was located there. This is not an exhaustive
review of all balancing or poset-correlation literature.

The absence of a search hit cannot exclude an older result under different
notation, an unstated classical corollary, a lemma in inaccessible full text,
or another exact implication of the atlas/FKG literature.

RECOMMENDED ATTRIBUTION AND STATUS

Safe wording:
  "We give an explicit derivation of the shared-downset swap inequality from
  marginal log-concavity and the Busemann–Ball theorem. Related geometric
  reductions for poset probabilities were developed by Kahn and Yu. The
  no-common-incomparable special case follows directly from Chan and Pak's
  first-two-position correlation inequality. We make no claim of novelty."

The most important unresolved bibliographic step is a lawful full-text
comparison against Kahn–Yu (1998), especially its two-dimensional geometric
lemmas. This is a literature-status caveat, not a gap in S108's independently
written derivation. No external writes, contacts, or publication were made.
