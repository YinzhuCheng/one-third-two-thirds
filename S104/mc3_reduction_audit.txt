1. SETUP AND TWO-DELETION FIBERS: PASS
The upsets must include their generators. Since every point outside {a,b} lies above x or y, a can have no predecessor: any such predecessor would force x<a or y<a. The same reasoning shows that b has exactly the predecessor a. In Q=P-{a,b}, the only minima are x and y. They are distinct, incomparable, and neither is above a or b.

For a fixed Q extension tau of length m, old slot i means immediately before tau_i, with m+1 the terminal slot. The first a-successor and b-successor ranks A,B satisfy 2<=A<=B<=m+1. The first inequality uses the fact that tau_1 is x or y; A<=B follows from successors(b) being a subset of successors(a).

Reinsertion gives a bijection with 1<=i<=A and i<=j<=B. Necessity follows from the successor restrictions and a<b. Conversely these conditions place a before every a-successor, b before every b-successor, and a before b; all remaining relations are already respected by tau. A common old slot has the unique order a,b. This proves N(A,B)=AB-A(A-1)/2 without deleting any dependence on the projection weight.

a<z is exactly i<=k_z, so its fiber count is F. b<z is exactly j<=k_z, so summing min(A,j) gives G. The order of x,y is unchanged by reinsertion, and x<y in Q iff tau starts with x, proving the last weighted identity. Thus every displayed marginal and E are computed under the same original law; using uniform tau instead would be wrong.

2. STRICT JOINT-SURVIVAL BOUND: PASS
Before either x or y occurs, only a,b can occur. Hence b<x and b<y is precisely the prefix a,b, with n=e(Q) completions. Every fiber has N>=3. If the first two Q labels are x,y in either order, neither is a successor of a or b, so A,B>=3 and N>=6. There are exactly 2d such fibers, d=e(Q-x-y)>=1, since both orders of the two minima can precede every extension of the remainder. In particular 2d<=n. Therefore
 E>=3(n-2d)+12d=3n+6d,
 T2=n/E<=n/(3n+6d)<1/3.
The proof includes the m=2 endpoint: d=1,n=2,E>=12. There is no width, CP or majority assumption hidden in strictness.

3. XYZ AND THE REMAINING BRANCH: PASS WITH A LOCAL SIGN REPAIR

5. TRUE CP BLOCKS AND CONDITIONING SCOPE: PASS
At j=0 and j=1 the residual minima are x,y,b_(j+1). The off-diagonal entry between an entry z and the neutral minimum is e(P_(j+1)-z)=C_(j+1,z). Deleting a can newly release exactly b while both entries are absent, giving the neutral diagonal E_2. Deleting b at j=1 releases no new label while x,y remain absent, giving diagonal zero. The remaining two diagonal entries are genuine release sums, not repeated deletions. Row sums yield V_j 1=C_j-C_(j+1) and 1^T C_j=E_j-E_(j+1), including j=2 if C_3=0 is understood.

These blocks count the first entry after neutral prefixes. They do not directly record all positions of a or b relative to x when y has occurred first; a post-y extension can contain additional y-side labels before x. S103's scalar closure therefore cannot simply be substituted for the missing full marginal. Applying its normalized theorem to u=b, comparator x, conditions on a<x, giving p_Q=Pr_P(b<x)/d, U_Q=U_P/d, d=Pr_P(a<x). The correct conclusion d U_P<=Pr_P(b<x)^2 is an upper bound on the successor event, with the original probability d retained.

The statement that width three gives 'chain constrained' y-side material is only a proposed interface, not an established general chain decomposition. Before both x and b appear, any activated y-side labels are incomparable with both absent x,b and hence form a chain. After b appears but before x, width three only forces width at most two for that material. A future proof must state which phase is being constrained.

6. ROOT-SWAP INJECTIONS AND THEIR INSUFFICIENCY: PASS
In relative type abxy the first three actual labels are a,b,x, so swapping b,x is legal and injective into type axby. In types axby and axyb the first two actual labels are a,x, so swapping them is legal and injective. The other displayed inequalities follow by exchanging x,y. Activated descendants explain why the maps need not be surjective.
