1. Setup and exact prefix simplification

Let Q be a finite poset of width at most 3, let u||y, and suppose their strict downsets both equal {d}. Let N be the points outside {d} union upward(u) union upward(y), with upward sets including u,y themselves. As established and audited in S102, N is a chain n_1<...<n_R. Each of its points is incomparable with u and y. No n_i<d is possible: otherwise n_i<u would contradict the given strict downset. The set of n_i above d is a suffix of the chain. Hence a uniquely determined k in {0,...,R} satisfies
  d||n_i for i<=k, and d<n_i for i>k.

For the induced prefix {d,n_1,...,n_j}, the only freedom is the slot occupied by d; that slot must precede n_(k+1) if this element is present. Thus exactly
  a_j=min(j+1,k+1),
  c_j=a_j-a_(j-1)=1 for 0<=j<=k, and 0 otherwise,
  f_j=sum_(i=0)^j a_i
     =(j+1)(j+2)/2                              (j<=k),
     =(k+1)(j+1)-k(k+1)/2                      (j>=k).
Here a_(-1)=0; the two formulas agree at k. No condition on successor relations beyond being a poset is added.

Let Q_j=Q-({d} union {n_1,...,n_j}), E_j=e(Q_j), q_j=Pr_(Q_j)(u<y). These are genuine residual counts and probabilities, not arbitrary parameters. The extension count of the ORIGINAL Q is
  E=sum_(j=0)^k E_j.
The original-law comparison probability is
  p=(sum_(j=0)^k E_j q_j)/E.
Both follow by the exact prefix-completion partition; they do not delete d and re-uniformize.
For every j<R, n_(j+1) is minimal in Q_j. Prepending it injects LE(Q_(j+1)) into LE(Q_j). Thus
  E_0>=E_1>=...>=E_R>0.
Indeed these inequalities are strict, since u and y are also minimal, but weak monotonicity suffices below.

2. Precise proved special class

THEOREM (conditional on the audited S102 energy bound).
In the above setup, if k<=3, then
  Pr_Q(some strict successor of u occurs before y) <= Pr_Q(u<y)^2.
The number R of neutral points, the number of successors, and all allowed successor relations are unrestricted. The hypothesis is at most three neutral points incomparable with d, not at most three neutral points in total.

Write U for the left probability. S102 equation (F), using exactly the real C_j,E_j and h=(1-p,-p), gives
  E(p^2-U) >= sum_(j=0)^(R-1) f_j E_j(q_j-q_(j+1))^2
               -sum_(j=0)^k E_j(q_j-p)^2.              (1)
It remains to prove a weighted path inequality supported on indices 0,...,k. The omitted terms j>=k in the energy are all nonnegative. We prove, for arbitrary real t_0,...,t_k and arbitrary positive nonincreasing masses m_0,...,m_k, that, when k<=3,
  sum_(i=0)^k m_i(t_i-bar_t)^2
    <= sum_(j=0)^(k-1) [(j+1)(j+2)/2]m_j(t_j-t_(j+1))^2,  (2)
where bar_t=(sum m_i t_i)/(sum m_i).
Apply (2) with m_i=E_i and t_i=q_i. The weighted mean is exactly p. Since R>=k, every retained edge exists whenever k>0. Equations (1),(2) give the theorem. When k=0 the variance is identically zero and (1) alone suffices, including R=0.

3. Exact proof of the path inequality

Put S=sum m_i and delta_j=t_j-t_(j+1). The standard pairwise identity, obtained by expanding both sides, is
  S sum_i m_i(t_i-bar_t)^2
    =sum_(0<=a<b<=k) m_a m_b (sum_(j=a)^(b-1) delta_j)^2.
Thus S times the right side of (2) minus S times its left side is delta^T B delta, where
  B_ij = S F_i m_i 1_(i=j)
           -(sum_(a=0)^min(i,j) m_a)(sum_(b=max(i,j)+1)^k m_b),
  F_i=(i+1)(i+2)/2.
This explicit formula has no stochastic or realizability assumptions.

For k=1, B=[m_0^2]>0.

For k=2, B has entries
  B_00=m_0^2,
  B_01=B_10=-m_0 m_2,
  B_11=3 S m_1-(m_0+m_1)m_2.
Its determinant is
  det(B)=m_0^2 S(3m_1-m_2)>0,
because m_1>=m_2>0. The leading entry is positive, so B is positive definite.

For k=3, its three leading principal minors are exactly
  D_1=m_0^2,
  D_2=m_0^2 S(3m_1-m_2-m_3),
  D_3=m_0^2 S^2(18m_1m_2-3m_1m_3-6m_2^2-5m_2m_3).
The second bracket is at least m_1>0. For the final bracket, m_3<=m_2<=m_1 gives
  18m_1m_2-3m_1m_3 >=15m_1m_2>=15m_2^2,
  -6m_2^2-5m_2m_3 >=-11m_2^2.
Hence the final bracket is at least 4m_2^2>0. By Sylvester's criterion B is positive definite. This proves (2) for k=3, and completes the special-class proof.

In all nonzero-dimensional cases equality in (2) occurs only when t_0=...=t_k. No claim about equality in the ultimate poset inequality is made, because (1) may have additional slack.

4. Why this does not close arbitrary k

The proof uses a scalar sufficient inequality valid for ALL directions when k<=3. It does not claim this scalar inequality for k>=4, nor replace the actual directions by arbitrary ones in the unresolved problem.
Already at k=4 the monotone-mass version of (2) is false, even for strictly decreasing log-concave positive masses. For an exact ABSTRACT example, take
  m=(104,103,102,101,100),
  t=(45,15,5,0,0), bar_t=449/34.
Then
  sum m_i(t_i-bar_t)^2=5011035/34,
  sum F_j m_j(t_j-t_(j+1))^2=139800,
  variance-energy=257835/34>0.
The masses are log-concave since each interior m_i^2-m_(i-1)m_(i+1)=1. These masses and directions are NOT claimed to arise from one poset. This is only an obstruction to extending (2) using monotonicity/log-concavity alone, and is not a counterexample to the square candidate. S102 already contains an actual-poset-weight scalar failure at k=8, with a direction not realizable by that poset.
