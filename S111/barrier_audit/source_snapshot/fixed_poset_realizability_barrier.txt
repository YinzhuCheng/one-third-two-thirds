S110: A decisive fixed-poset realizability barrier
2026-10-10. Exact counterexample to a proposed GENERIC ANALYTIC proof route.
This is NOT a poset counterexample. The dimension-sharp poset target stays OPEN.

1. Two genuine fibers in dimension m=5
Use a seven-element poset with roots u,y and five deletion vertices
A1,A2,B1,B2,B3. Let P1 have only the relations u<A1,A2 and y<B1,B2,B3.
Let P2 have only u<A1 and y<B1,B2,B3, so A2 is neutral.
Both deletion posets are the five-element antichain. Their exact unnormalized
Lebesgue fiber functions from S108 are
  f1(s,t)=(1-s)^2(1-t)^3,
  f2(s,t)=(1-s)(1-t)^3,
on the unit square. P1 is sharp at n=7; P2 satisfies the inequality strictly.

2. A positive mixture violates the dimension-sharp inequality
Set
  g=(5/6)f1+(1/6)f2
   =(5/6)(1-s)(6/5-s)(1-t)^3.
The exact sector and diagonal integrals are
  F=37/1008, G=3/56, E=13/144, h=8/315.
Multiplying by 7!=5040 gives INTEGERS
  F*=185, G*=270, E*=455, H*=128,
  Ru*=57, Ry*=142.
Nevertheless
  6 E* H* - 7 F* G* = -210 < 0.
The ordinary Ball square still holds: H*^2-Ru*Ry*=8290>0.
The labels E*,F*,G*,H* are integer-valued integral quantities of g, not actual
extension counts of a poset.

3. The mixture meets all of the previously proposed analytic conditions
- Exact unit-square support, positive in the open square.
- Coordinatewise decreasing, with vanishing top and right boundary values.
- MTP2, with equality, because it is a product of one-variable functions.
- Its fifth root is concave: up to a positive constant it is the weighted
  geometric mean x^(1/5)(x+1/5)^(1/5)y^(3/5), where x=1-s,y=1-t.
  The weights sum to 1, so concavity follows by the usual concavity of the
  weighted geometric mean composed with affine functions.
- It is a single global polynomial of total degree 5, with no interior knots.
- On the sector s<=t, put x=s,y=t-s,z=1-t. Its homogeneous polynomial is
    (y+z)(x+6y+6z)z^3/6,
  which has nonnegative coefficients. The transposed sector has the same
  property by expanding the corresponding nonnegative affine factors.
Thus sector polynomiality and nonnegative simplex-Bernstein coefficients do
not rescue the generic analytic route.

4. Even an integer order-statistic-kernel histogram does not suffice
The two true fibers have rank-pair histograms on the 120 deletion extensions.
Their 5:1 mixture has the following NONNEGATIVE INTEGER histogram, summing to120:
  (1,2):33, (1,3):11, (2,1):33, (2,3):1, (3,1):23,
  (3,2):1, (4,1):13, (4,2):1, (5,1):3, (5,2):1.
Here (p,q) are the nested-spacing widths in the true order-statistic kernels.
Integrating these kernels gives exactly the integer quantities in Section 2.
Therefore nonnegative/integer kernel coefficients and integer final counts
also do not imply the dimension-sharp inequality.

5. Why this does not contradict the poset conjecture
The mixture cannot be the fiber of any SINGLE seven-element poset/pair.
If it were, g(0,0)=1 would imply vol O(P-{u,y})=1, hence the deletion poset
would have to be the five-element antichain. If the common downset were
nonempty, both successor sets would have to be empty (otherwise the deletion
poset would contain a predecessor-successor comparison). Then A=B and f
would be symmetric in s,t, whereas g is not. Thus the common downset is empty.
For an antichain deletion poset and empty downset,
  f(s,0)=(1-s)^r
for the integer number r of strict u-successors. But
  g(s,0)=(5/6)(1-s)^2+(1/6)(1-s)
is not a pure integer power. Contradiction.

Consequently the unresolved proof must exploit rank-histogram compatibility
for one fixed poset/pair, not merely positive mixtures of allowed kernels or
analytic features shared by individual fibers.
