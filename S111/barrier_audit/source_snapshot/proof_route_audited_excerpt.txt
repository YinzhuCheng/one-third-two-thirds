Definitions for this excerpt: P is a finite n-element poset, n>=2, and
u,y are distinct incomparable elements with the same strict downset.
E=e(P) counts all complete linear extensions. F counts extensions in which
u precedes y; G counts the opposite order. H counts extensions in which
u precedes y and the literal exchange of their positions is also a linear
extension. Thus E=F+G, Ru=F-H and Ry=G-H. Strict successor sets contain all
strict successors, not just covers. All counts use the uniform original
complete-extension law. This paragraph is a publication introduction;
the two numbered sections below are exact source excerpts.

1. Equality classification in the known only-two-minima subcase
Suppose the only minimal elements of P are u,y. Then F=e(P-u),
G=e(P-y), and H=e(P-u-y). Normalized Fishburn gives the target.
The equality criterion is also known: equality holds iff u and y are in
distinct connected components of the comparability graph. Indeed apply
Theorem 4.5 of Chan--Pak, Equality conditions for correlation inequalities,
arXiv:2607.06275, with lower ideals A={u}, B={y} and upper ideals C=D=empty.
Every connected component of a finite poset contains a minimal element.
Therefore if u,y are the only minima, the condition says exactly that P is
the disjoint union of two rooted posets, with unique minima u and y.
This is only a classification inside this already-proved subcase.

7. Elementary sharp theorem and equality classification when one wing vanishes
This is a genuine restricted proof of the target, independent of Ball/Fishburn.
Let Aadj count extensions with u immediately before y. From any reverse
extension (y before u), delete u and reinsert it immediately before y. The
result is valid: every predecessor of u is a predecessor of y and has already
occurred, while moving u earlier cannot violate a successor relation.
Given the result and the old rank of u, the input is uniquely recoverable.
If u has rank k in the output, there are at most n-k<=n-1 inputs. Therefore
  G <= (n-1) Aadj <= (n-1) F.
Exchanging labels also gives F<=(n-1)G. In particular each orientation has
probability at least 1/n under the original uniform extension law.

If Ru=0, then F=H and E=F+G, so
  (n-1)EH-nFG = F[(n-1)F-G] >=0.
If Ry=0 use the symmetric argument. Nested strict successor sets force one
wing to vanish, so this covers every pair with nested successor sets.

Equality in the Ru=0 case is also elementary. It forces equality in both
injection bounds, hence every forward extension has u immediately before y,
u has rank 1 there, and all n-1 possible old ranks yield valid inverse moves.
Moving u to rank n must be legal, so u has no successors. The rank-1 condition
implies D is empty. Thus u is isolated. A third minimal element z would allow
an extension beginning z,u,y, a contradiction. Hence P-u has unique minimum y.
Conversely the disjoint union of isolated u and an arbitrary rooted poset
with unique minimum y attains equality. The Ry=0 case is the label swap.
This includes n=2 and is exactly the size-one-arm part of the proposed global
rooted-disjoint equality family.
