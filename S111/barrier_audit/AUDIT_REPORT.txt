2. Definitions and normalization used throughout

The pair u,y has equal strict downsets D and is therefore incomparable.
For Q=P-{u,y}, let Omega=O(Q) be its order polytope with unnormalized ordinary
Lebesgue measure, L=max_{d in D} z_d (L=0 if D is empty), and let T_u,T_y be
the minimum strict-successor coordinate (1 if the corresponding set is empty).
The fiber is f(s,t)=vol{z in Omega:T_u-L>=s,T_y-L>=t}.

Let F_int and G_int be its two sector integrals, and H_int=int_0^1 t f(t,t)dt.
For a genuine n-element poset, n! times these integrals equal integers F,G,H:
F counts extensions with u before y; G counts the opposite orientation; H
counts the forward extensions still valid after literally swapping u and y.
To see the last identity, the permissible swappable-forward insertion region
in one deletion fiber is 0<=s<t<=min(T_u-L,T_y-L); its area is half that
minimum squared. Integrating the diagonal survival function gives that area.
Thus both the original normalization proof and the added ray lemma refer to
actual unnormalized fibers and these exact integer extension counts.

4. Exact fixed-poset nonrealizability: source argument passes

For a genuine seven-point fiber, f(0,0)=vol O(Q)=e(Q)/5!. The value g(0,0)=1
therefore forces e(Q)=120, so Q is the five-point antichain.

If D is nonempty, any successor of either u or y would be strictly above an
element of D by transitivity, contradicting that Q is an antichain. Both
successor sets must therefore be empty. Then T_u=T_y=1 and the fiber is
symmetric, whereas g is not. Hence D is empty in any putative realization.

With D empty and Q an antichain, the boundary f(s,0) equals (1-s)^r, where r
is the integer number of strict u-successors. The mixture has boundary
(5/6)(1-s)^2+(1/6)(1-s), whose derivative at 0 is -11/6, not an integer.
This is a contradiction. There is no normalization or conditional-volume
substitution hidden in this argument.

An independent even shorter boundary check avoids splitting off symmetry:
for an antichain Q and D nonempty, the boundary is (1-s)^|D|, because both
successor sets are empty. For D empty, it is (1-s)^r as above. Thus in every
case f(1/2,0) belongs to {1,1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16,1/32}, whereas g(1/2,0)=7/24.

This audit enumerated all 1055 labeled shared-downset possibilities with
antichain Q: 4^5 arbitrary successor-membership assignments when D is empty,
and 2^5-1 nonempty choices of D, with both successor sets empty. None has the
required boundary value. The conceptual argument already proves completeness.

5. Additional audited lemma: positive-ray nonrealizability at n=7

The preceding f(0,0)=1 argument alone does not exclude lambda*g. The following
separate integrality argument does.

Suppose f=lambda*g is the unnormalized fiber of a seven-element shared-downset
pair, where lambda>0. The actual counts must satisfy
  F=185 lambda and H=128 lambda.
Because 9*185-13*128=1, it follows that
  lambda=9F-13H
is an integer. But lambda=f(0,0)=vol O(Q) lies in (0,1]. Hence lambda=1,
which has just been excluded.

Equivalently, testing the 120 candidate volumes e(Q)/120 leaves only lambda=1
when both scaled F and H are integral. This proof excludes the entire
positive ray at fixed n=7 without assuming the sharp inequality, and without
an exhaustive search of all seven-element posets. It makes no claim about
changing n or replacing the fiber by a different normalized statistic.

6. Restricted sharp theorem and equality classification

For any reverse extension, remove u and insert it immediately before y.
Equal strict downsets guarantee legality: all predecessors of u already lie
before y; moving u earlier cannot break a strict-successor constraint.
The original extension is determined by the resulting adjacent-forward
extension and u's old rank. If u has output rank k, at most n-k old ranks
are possible. Thus
  G <= (n-1) A_adj <= (n-1) F,
and exchanging labels proves F<=(n-1)G.

If Ru=F-H=0, then
  (n-1)EH-nFG = F[(n-1)F-G] >= 0.
The Ry=0 case is symmetric. Nested successor sets force one wing to vanish:
for example U(u) subset U(y) forbids a u-successor strictly between u and y.

Equality when Ru=0 forces both injection bounds to be equalities. Every
forward extension is adjacent, u has rank 1 in each, and every possible old
rank, including n, yields a legal inverse insertion. Rank 1 gives D empty;
legal movement to rank n gives no u-successors. Thus u is isolated. A third
minimal element z would give an extension beginning z,u,y, contradicting the
rank-1 condition. Hence P-u has unique minimum y.

Conversely, if u is isolated and P-u has unique minimum y, an extension of
P-u permits all n insertion positions of u. Only the first position puts u
before y; it is swappable. Thus F=H=e(P-u), G=(n-1)e(P-u), giving equality.
Label swapping gives the complete Ry=0 classification. The n=2 case agrees.

Independent exhaustive tests generated each naturally labeled poset by adding
a valid predecessor ideal and counted actual complete extensions and swaps:
  n=2: 2 posets, 1 shared pair, 1 one-wing pair, 1 equality.
  n=3: 7 posets, 8 shared pairs, 8 one-wing pairs, 3 equalities.
  n=4: 40 posets, 73 shared pairs, 70 one-wing pairs, 8 equalities.
  n=5: 357 posets, 899 shared pairs, 813 one-wing pairs, 35 equalities.
  n=6: 4824 posets, 15521 shared pairs, 13122 one-wing pairs, 240 equalities.
Both insertion bounds, nested-successor implications, and the exact equality
classification passed in every case. This is not a new claimed exhaustive
range for the general conjecture.

7. Other proof-route checks and source attribution

The empty-common-downset MTP2 sublattice proof is valid. With a unique maximal
common predecessor d, the change w=-z_d, x_v=z_v-z_d, x_d=0 has absolute
Jacobian 1. All transformed constraints, including w<=0<=w+1, are difference
inequalities and define a sublattice. Thus the quoted marginal-closure theorem
applies. The multiple-maximal-predecessor obstruction is genuinely different.

The explicit eight-element poset with predecessor masks [0,0,3,3,1,2,23,43]
was independently counted: E=200, F=G=100, H=74, sharp defect=23600>0.
Its stated sector polynomial reproduces f00=1/40, first derivatives -1/120,
mixed derivative zero, and the exact negative epsilon=1/100 MTP2 determinant
-24895078440973240401/6400000000000000000000000000. Thus this is an actual
counterexample to general fiber MTP2, not to the dimension-sharp swap bound.
The earlier analytic m=3 square-support example also reintegrates to defect
-1/3600. Other omitted analytic examples are outside this published bundle.

The only-two-minima equality result remains credited to Chan--Pak,
Equality conditions for correlation inequalities, arXiv:2607.06275, Theorem
4.5, exactly as in the supplied proof note. Its reduction to two disjoint
uniquely rooted components is correct: every comparability component contains
a minimal element. No fresh external source fetch was made in this audit,
and the cited theorem is not presented as an original result of this work.
