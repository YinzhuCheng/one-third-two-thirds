1. EXACT STATEMENT AND STRUCTURE

The ground set consists of the disjoint labels
  a,b,c_1,...,c_m; y,z_1,...,z_r; x,
with generators
  a<b<c_1<...<c_m, y<z_1<...<z_r,
  a<z_1, x<z_s, x<c_1,
and no further relations except those forced by transitivity.
The total number of elements is n=m+r+4. Both tail lengths must be positive,
and the attachment index satisfies 1<=s<=r; all subsequent ranges use this.
The three displayed chains, including singleton x, give width at most three;
a,x,y are three distinct minimal elements, so the width is exactly three.
The strict predecessor set of b is {a}. Every c_i lies above x and every
label on the other long chain lies above y. Neither a nor b lies in the
root-inclusive upsets of x or y. Hence the avoidance set is exactly {a<b}.
All five proposed comparison pairs are incomparable in this poset.

Under the uniform distribution on complete linear extensions, the assertion is:
  Pr(a<x)>2/3 and Pr(a<y)>2/3
  imply that Pr(b<x) or Pr(b<y) belongs to [1/3,2/3].
The argument never assumes a particular value or orientation for Pr(x<y).
It therefore establishes the stated five-pair menu conclusion with a strictly
smaller menu in this special family, under the two stated majority hypotheses.
It does not establish this implication for every poset with avoidance set {a<b}.

2. INDEPENDENT RECONSTRUCTION OF EVERY PREFIX CELL

Put M=m+2, R=r+1, N=M+R. N does not include the singleton x.
For a complete extension let i,j be the numbers of first-chain and second-chain
labels occurring before x. Since x<c_1 and x<z_s, necessarily
  0<=i<=2, 0<=j<=s.
If i=0, at most the single label y can precede x: z_1 would require a first.
Thus the permissible cells are (0,0),(0,1), and (1,j),(2,j) for 0<=j<=s.
There are exactly 2s+4 cells, specializing to eight cells at s=2.

Define
  U_j=binom(N-1-j,M-1), 0<=j<=s+1,
  V_j=binom(N-2-j,M-2), 0<=j<=s.
Pascal's identity gives V_j=U_j-U_(j+1).
For i=1 or 2, after the prefix and x have occurred, every cross-chain
constraint is satisfied. The two remaining chains may therefore interleave
arbitrarily: their counts are U_j for i=1, V_j for i=2.

Here is the independently reconstructed table. Each count includes the complete
suffix; the last two columns count complete extensions satisfying the comparison.

  cell          total                 a<y                    b<y
  (0,0)         U0+U1                 U0                     V0
  (0,1)         U1                    0                      0
  (1,0)         U0                    U0                     V0
  (2,0)         V0                    V0                     V0
  (1,j),j>=1    2U_j                  U_j                    0
  (2,j),j>=1    (2j+1)V_j             (j+1)V_j                V_j

For (0,0), x is first. The next label is either a, allowing U0 suffixes,
or y, after which a is forced, allowing U1 suffixes. The a<y event selects
the first case. Within that case b<y forces b next and leaves V0 suffixes.
For (0,1), the beginning is y,x,a, where a is forced after x; there are U1
remaining interleavings and both a<y and b<y are false.
For (1,0), the beginning is a,x. Requiring b<y makes b the next label and
leaves V0 suffixes. The (2,0) cell begins a,b,x, with both comparisons true.
For (1,j), j>=1, only the two prefixes beginning a,y or y,a are possible.
This remains true at j=1; for j>=2 it follows from a<z_1. Exactly the first
prefix has a<y, while y precedes b in either case.
For (2,j), j>=1, a is first or second in the prefix. If a is first, inserting
b among the j ordered second-chain labels gives j+1 prefixes. If a is second,
the first label is y; inserting b among the remaining j-1 ordered labels gives
j prefixes. These sets are disjoint, totaling 2j+1. Exactly j+1 have a<y,
and exactly the single prefix beginning a,b has b<y.

This is a bijective count of actual complete extensions. No deletion projection
is re-uniformized, and no assumption about an arbitrary prefix-weight law enters.

3. ALL SIX COUNT FORMULAS AND COUPLINGS

Let E be the total number of complete extensions and K_uv count those with u<v.
Set
  C=U0+2U1, I=U0+2 sum_(j=1)^s U_j,
  u=U_(s+1), T=(2s+1)u.
The two i=0 cells contribute C. The i=1 cells contribute I.
The i=2 cells telescope as
  V0+sum_(j=1)^s(2j+1)V_j = I-T.
Consequently
  E=C+2I-T, K_ax=2I-T, K_bx=I-T.
The three j=0 cells sum to 3U0, proving K_xy=3U0.
The b<y column gives
  K_by=3V0+sum_(j=1)^s V_j=3U0-2U1-u.
The a<y column gives
  K_ay=3U0-U1+sum_(j=1)^s[U_j+(j+1)V_j]
      =2U0+I-(s+1)u.
These independent sums agree with every formula in the frozen proof.
In particular,
  2K_bx-K_ax=-T,
  K_by=2K_ay-E.
Both identities are exact family-specific facts.
Because C>0, K_ax=E-C<E. Because T>0,
  2K_bx=K_ax-T<E,
so Pr(b<x)<1/2 for every member, without any majority premise.
Thus Pr(b<x)>=1/3 immediately settles the desired closed balanced interval.
If Pr(a<y)>2/3, the second identity gives Pr(b<y)>1/3 strictly, again with
no need to assume anything about the value of Pr(b<x).

4. INVERSE-RATIO AND STRICT UPPER-BOUND AUDIT

We now examine the only remaining case K_bx<E/3. Let
  S=sum_(j=2)^s U_j,
where S=0 when s=1. Then I=C+2S. Direct subtraction gives
  3K_ax-2E=4S-T,
  3K_bx-E=2(S-T).
The strict first-majority premise and current strict case therefore give
  T/4<S<T.
As T>0, this rules out s=1. Hence all divisions by s-1 below are legal.

The complete U range is valid even at the far attachment s=r:
  N-1-j >= M-1 for j<=s+1<=R.
Thus every U_j is positive, including U_(s+1)=1 when s=r.
For 0<=j<=s, R-j>=1 and
  U_j/U_(j+1)=(N-1-j)/(R-j)
             =1+(M-1)/(R-j)>1.
These inverse ratios increase with j, since M-1=m+1>0. Equivalently, the
positive sequence is log-concave in this range; it is the INCREASING inverse
ratio, rather than the opposite inequality, that is used in the proof.
The ratio U_s/u is at least U0/U1. Since the U sequence is decreasing,
  S >= (s-1)U_s >= (s-1)(U0/U1)u.
Combining with the strict upper bound S<(2s+1)u yields
  U0/U1 < (2s+1)/(s-1)=2+3/(s-1)<=5.
Strictness is preserved by S<T, even when the final bound equals five at s=2.

Finally,
  2E-3K_by=-3U0+18U1+8S+(1-4s)u.
A useful equivalent positive decomposition is
  2E-3K_by
    =3U1+3u + 3(5U1-U0) + 2(4S-T).
Both parenthesized terms are strictly positive by the inequalities just proved.
Since U1,u>0, this proves
  2E-3K_by>3U1+3u>0.
Together with K_by>E/3, it yields the strict rescue
  1/3<Pr(b<y)<2/3 whenever Pr(b<x)<1/3
under the two original strict majority hypotheses. This proves the theorem for
all permitted parameters. The argument is elementary and self-contained; no
external log-concavity theorem is required.

5. ENDPOINTS, STRICTNESS, AND LOGICAL SCOPE

- s=1 is a valid poset/counting endpoint. Here S=0 and 3K_ax-2E=-T<0,
  so the first majority hypothesis is impossible. The theorem is vacuous there,
  not an undefined expression involving s-1.
- r=1 forces s=1 and is therefore covered by that endpoint.
- s=r is valid. In particular U_(s+1)=1; no nonexistent chain label or binomial
  coefficient with negative admissible range is used.
- m=1 is valid. All factorials/binomial coefficients and suffix counts remain
  positive. The proof requires no hidden lower bound such as m>=2.
- Pr(b<x)=1/3 is settled in the first case. No strict upper-rescue argument is
  needed at this boundary, and it is never incorrectly excluded as unbalanced.
- The two a-majority assumptions are strictly greater than 2/3. The proof does
  not claim the same strict rescued interval from non-strict premises.
- The identity K_by=2K_ay-E and the two-chain suffix law are used only for this
  explicitly generated poset. Width three and a neutral chain alone do not
  establish those identities. No conclusion for arbitrary MC3 follows.
- HALF and L remain refuted by their existing examples; the present theorem
  explains why those examples do not refute MC3. It does not reinstate either
  one-sided shortcut.
