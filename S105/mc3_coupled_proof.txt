S105 — The proposed two-step MC3 fixed menu is FALSE
A self-contained binomial counting proof for the 72-element counterexample
Date: 2026-10-10.
Status: SELF-CONTAINED FINITE COUNTEREXAMPLE PROOF; an independent reading reviewed the binomial counts, insertion weights, and swap bijection and found the stated counting argument valid.

RESULT FIRST
There exists a width-three poset with minimal x,y and
  P \ (up(x) union up(y)) = {a<b},
with P(a<x)>2/3 and P(a<y)>2/3, for which none of
  (a,x), (a,y), (b,x), (b,y), (x,y)
is balanced. All five probabilities below use the same original uniform complete-linear-extension law.
Thus the requested coupled rescue is itself false. The surviving high-side pattern q=P(b<x)<1/3, r=P(b<y)>2/3 genuinely occurs. It is not merely an artificial relative-order law, a mixture, or a counterexample to a stronger sufficient lemma.
This refutes this precise fixed-menu two-step MC3 statement. It does not refute the 1/3–2/3 conjecture, WIDTH3, MAIN33, or any differently quantified claim called MC3. Indeed, an explicit balanced pair outside the menu is exhibited in section 5.

1. THE POSET, INCLUDING EVERY GENERATING RELATION
Use the three disjoint chains
  x<u,
  y<z_1<...<z_13,
  a<b<c_1<...<c_54,
and add precisely the three cross-chain relations
  a<z_1,  x<c_1,  c_50<z_8.
Take transitive closure and impose no other relations.
There are 2+14+56=72 elements. These chains cover P, so width(P)<=3. The labels a,x,y are a three-antichain, so width(P)=3. They are all minimal. The only strict predecessor of b is a.
Every c_i and u is above x; every y,z_i is above y. Neither a nor b is above x or y. Hence the displayed avoidance set is literally {a<b}, with upward sets including their generators. Each of the five menu pairs is incomparable in P.

2. GENERAL FINITE BINOMIAL FORMULA, NOT AN INTERPOLATED FIT
For integers m>=1, r>=1, 1<=h<=m, 1<=t<=r, use the same construction with c_1,...,c_m, z_1,...,z_r and cross relations
  a<z_1, x<c_1, c_h<z_t.
Let M=m+2, R=r+1, N=M+R. Thus the two long chains have lengths M,R, and P has N+2=m+r+5 elements. The concrete example uses (m,r,h,t)=(54,13,50,8), so (M,R,N)=(56,14,70).
First erase the leaf u from a complete extension. In the resulting extension, let i be the number of a-chain elements before x, and j the number of y-chain elements before x. Since x<c_1, i belongs to {0,1,2}. Since x<c_h<z_t, j belongs to {0,...,t}. The relation a<z_1 forbids i=0,j>=2. All other cells in this index set are possible.

Define S(i,j), for the suffixes used below, as the number of interleavings of the remaining two chains after i a-chain elements and j y-chain elements have been removed, subject only to c_h<z_t. Write
  alpha=M-i, beta=R-j, H=h+2-i, T=t+1-j.
For H,T>=1,
  S(i,j) = sum from k=0 to T-1 of
     binom(H+k-1,k) binom(alpha+beta-H-k,beta-k).       (1)
Proof: classify a suffix by the number k of y-chain elements before its H-th a-chain element. That H-th a-chain element is c_h. The gate c_h<z_t is exactly k<T. Before c_h there are H-1 a-chain and k y-chain elements, in binom(H+k-1,k) orders; after c_h the remaining two chains freely interleave, giving the second factor. These classes are disjoint and exhaustive. If H=0, the gate has already been passed, and S(i,j)=binom(alpha+beta,alpha).

For a cell (i,j), define raw counts C_ij, A_ij, B_ij for, respectively, its total size before reinserting u, its a<y count, and its b<y count. They are as follows:
  i=0,j=0:
    C_00=S(1,0)+S(1,1), A_00=S(1,0), B_00=S(2,0).
  i=0,j=1:
    C_01=S(1,1), A_01=B_01=0.
  i=1,j=0:
    C_10=A_10=S(1,0), B_10=S(2,0).
  i=2,j=0:
    C_20=A_20=B_20=S(2,0).
  i=1,1<=j<=t:
    C_1j=2 S(1,j), A_1j=S(1,j), B_1j=0.
  i=2,1<=j<=t:
    C_2j=(2j+1) S(2,j), A_2j=(j+1) S(2,j), B_2j=S(2,j).       (2)

Here is a direct proof of every case in (2). After x has appeared and after a has appeared, all cross constraints except c_h<z_t have been satisfied, so S counts the suffix. With i=1,j>=1, the legal prefixes before x are exactly a,y,z_1,... and y,a,z_1,...; one has a<y and neither has b<y. With i=2,j>=1, a can occur before y or immediately after y. In the first case b has j+1 possible positions in the y-chain prefix, and in the second case it has j possible positions. Thus there are 2j+1 prefixes, j+1 with a<y and one with b<y. The j=0 cases follow by taking the displayed initial a-chain prefix. In (1,0), asking b<y makes the first suffix label b, leaving S(2,0).
For (0,0), x is first. The next label of the two long chains is either a, leaving S(1,0), or y followed necessarily by a, leaving S(1,1). The event a<y selects the first branch, and b<y forces a,b as the first two suffix labels, leaving S(2,0). In (0,1), the prefix is y,x and a must be the next long-chain label, leaving S(1,1). This proves the table without any probability renormalization.

Finally reinsert u. Its only order requirement is x<u. There are exactly
  w_ij=N-i-j+1                                                   (3)
slots after x in each erased extension from cell (i,j). Hence the cell contributes w_ij C_ij complete extensions of P, w_ij A_ij with a<y, and w_ij B_ij with b<y. The factor is constant within the cell, not constant across cells; retaining it is essential.
Writing K_vw for the original number of complete extensions with v<w, we therefore have exactly
  E=e(P)=sum w_ij C_ij,
  K_ax=sum over i>=1 of w_ij C_ij,
  K_ay=sum w_ij A_ij,
  K_bx=sum over i=2 of w_ij C_ij,
  K_by=sum w_ij B_ij,
  K_xy=sum over j=0 of w_ij C_ij.                              (4)
Equations (1)–(4) are a bijective counting proof, valid before any numerical evaluation. They neither enumerate order ideals nor use the search implementation.

  K_ay=117303434549811,
  K_bx=45491483154535,
  K_by=97129099799325,

The probabilities, for orientation only, are approximately

The exact strict-majority margins are
  3K_ax-2E=275420649591>0,
  3K_ay-2E=76954765048839>0,
  3K_by-2E=16431760797381>0,
  3K_xy-2E=45925286375082>0.
The exact minority margin is
  E-3K_bx=1003319836692>0.
Consequently both required a comparisons exceed 2/3, the entry orientation x<y exceeds 2/3, b<x is below 1/3, and b<y exceeds 2/3. Every menu comparison is strictly outside the closed balanced interval [1/3,2/3]. No numerical precision or boundary convention enters this conclusion.

4. A COUPLING IDENTITY SURVIVES BUT DOES NOT RESCUE THE MENU
In this poset family,
  K_by=2K_ay-E.                                               (5)
There is a direct original-law involution proving (5). Exchanging the actual labels a and y maps the extensions with y<a bijectively onto the extensions with a<y<b.
For the forward map, between y and a there cannot be a y-successor: every strict y-successor is above a. Moving y later therefore causes no violation; moving a earlier cannot violate a predecessor because a is minimal. The image has a<y<b since b originally followed a. For the inverse map, between a and y there cannot be any a-successor: every strict a-successor is either b or above b, or a strict y-successor; the former are excluded by y<b and the latter by their requirement to follow y. Thus exchanging a and y again is legal.
The two disjoint parts of {a<y} are {b<y} and {a<y<b}, and the latter has the same size as {y<a}; (5) follows. In the certificate, 2K_ay-E is exactly 97129099799325.
This identity forces b<y>1/3 from a<y>2/3, but it equally forces b<y>2/3 when a<y>5/6. Here a<y is approximately 0.85325>5/6. Thus a correct lower-side coupling can coexist with a genuine high-side failure. The family identity is not a missing universal upper rescue bound.

5. A BALANCED PAIR OUTSIDE THE MENU
The pair (c_1,y) is incomparable. To have c_1 before y, a cell must have j=0. In each of i=0,1,2 the remaining initial a-chain labels through c_1 must precede y, after which there are S(3,0) possible suffixes. Reinserting u gives the respective factors N+1,N,N-1. Therefore
  K_c1y=3N S(3,0)=79885668970800.
In the same original law,
  3K_c1y-E=102179237612103>0,
  2E-3K_c1y=35298531688194>0.
Hence P(c_1<y) is approximately 0.5810806313, strictly balanced. This concretely demonstrates that the counterexample concerns the five-label selection menu, not the existence of a balanced pair in this poset.

6. SCOPE, KNOWN INPUTS, AND REMAINING BOUNDARIES
This proof is self-contained finite counting. It needs no CP theorem, XYZ inequality, S103 square theorem, asymptotic estimate, or finite-search extrapolation. Discovery was made by a separate S105 exact search. This author reconstructed the complete counts from the independent binomial partition above.
The S103 normalized square and the S104 original-law reductions remain valid at their stated interfaces. In particular, the earlier reduction to q<1/3 and either r<1/3 or r>2/3 is compatible with this example; this example realizes its second residual region. No legitimate theorem at those interfaces can imply the now-refuted full menu claim without an additional hypothesis.
The implication ruling out the low-low region may still merit separate study. It is not proved or refuted by this high-side example. A trial unconditional linear inequality 3P(a<x)+2P(x<y)-P(b<y)<=3 was independently refuted by an independent search; the finite deadline-fiber evidence that motivated it must not be used as a theorem.
The specialized two-tail rescue theorem proved separately in S105 has independently passed audit. The present counterexample does satisfy x<z_8, by the path x<c_50<z_8. It lies outside that theorem’s precisely defined family because of its extra x-successor u and additional remote gate c_50<z_8. Merely retaining an x<z_s relation is not sufficient to invoke the special-family theorem. MAIN33, WIDTH3 and general existence of a balanced pair remain OPEN here.
